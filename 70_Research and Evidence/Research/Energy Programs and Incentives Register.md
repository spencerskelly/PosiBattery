# Energy Programs and Incentives Register

## Definition

Public evidence on the regulations, rebates, vouchers, credits and tax incentives that have pushed or paid for the move from combustion and lead-acid forklifts toward electric, lithium and fuel cell trucks, and on the published outcomes of those programs. Built 2026-10-18 from 13 public sources, all read through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand). Feeds the demand side of [[Market Overview]] and the cost inputs of [[Fleet Energy and Cost Evidence Register]]. All data is from public sources.

Derived checks (assumptions stated):

- Mass Save's USD 6,000 against the CenterPoint page's illustrative electric-over-combustion price gap of USD 5,000 (USD 30,000 against USD 25,000) is 120%. The two figures come from different utilities and the gap is an illustration, so this is only a scale check.
- SRP's USD 2,000 conversion rebate is 40% of the same USD 5,000 gap (derived, same caveat).
- Liberty medium case: 532 + 174 = 706 forklifts; delivery USD 1,852,365 is USD 2,624 per forklift against a USD 2,500 incentive; all-in program cost USD 5,124 per forklift (derived). Delivery cost is 51% of the total budget (derived).
- Liberty kWh: 14,523,600 / 532 = 27,300 kWh per conventional forklift and 4,367,400 / 174 = 25,100 kWh per rapid-charge forklift, identical across the low, medium and high cases (derived), so the model uses a fixed per-truck figure.

### 3. Energy side: charger regulation and load

- The ETCC project (2008 to 2009) states California had about 275,000 large chargers using over 4,200 GWh a year, or 15.3 MWh per charger (derived); it reports no savings figure. [[Document - ETCC Forklift Battery Charger project (PG&E PGE 0808)|etc]]
- Compare the Fleet Energy and Cost Evidence Register: EPRI puts annual energy at 12,960 to 25,932 kWh per electric truck. The ETCC per-charger average (15.3 MWh) falls inside that range if one charger serves one truck, but the ETCC scope (forklifts, airport equipment, neighborhood electric vehicles, golf carts) is wider, so it is a consistency check only.
- Forklift chargers are state-regulated in California (above 2 kW) while smaller chargers fall under the federal standard. The CEC page gives no numeric limits in the text read. [[Document - CEC battery charger standards page (Title 20 and federal)|cec]]
- Utility programs reward efficiency features: Mass Save requires charger efficiency of at least 90% and replacement of SCR or ferroresonant units; SRP requires at least 89%, network enablement and demand-response capability. [[Document - Mass Save forklift electrification rebate (2026)|mas]], [[Document - SRP E-Tech Forklift Program flyer (FY23)|srp]]
- Plug Power's deck says 25 to 30% of distribution center electricity is lead-acid battery charging during regular hours; the basis and year are not stated and the source is a fuel cell seller. [[Document - Plug Power Investor Deck 2026 (April 2026)|plg]]

### 7. What this changes in the market view

- California's mandate is the largest single demand driver on record in these sources (89,000 trucks), and it sets dates (2026, 2028 to 2038) that can be used in the outlook scenarios of [[Market Overview]].
- Voucher and utility money is small per truck against the electric-over-combustion price gap, and CORE's forklift allocation is USD 5M shared with TRU; the evidence does not show incentives alone moving national volumes.
- Fuel cell demand is the segment most exposed to federal credit changes (30C and 45W dates above; 48E not verified).
