# Document - Oregon DEQ Forklift Electricity Estimation Discussion Draft (2023-05-24)

## Definition

Oregon DEQ's unapproved May 2023 discussion draft for estimating forklift charging electricity until metering became mandatory.

## Notes

**How it was read:** through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand), not the raw file.

Added for the western-states clean fuel topic. A DEQ discussion draft (version 2023-05-24) of a transitional estimation method for forklift charging electricity. It states that it "cannot be used for reporting in the Clean Fuels Program" and that DEQ has not approved it, so it records a proposal, not a rule.

- **Formula:** kWh per forklift per charge cycle = depth of discharge x battery capacity rating x charger efficiency rating x charge return factor; per quarter multiply by shifts per day and work days per quarter; sum across forklifts.
- **Inputs:** depth of discharge capped at 20 percent of battery capacity; at most two charge cycles per day; charger efficiency 82 to 86 percent (ferro-resonant), 78 to 82 percent (SCR), 90 to 96 percent (high frequency), older chargers toward the low end; charge return factor about 115 percent (ferro-resonant and high frequency) and about 118 percent (SCR); forklifts split into pre-2016 and post-2016.
- **Dates:** webinar 2023-05-30; comments due 2023-06-06; if approved, usable for the Q1 and Q2 2023 reports, and "starting with the Q3 2023 report, only metered data will be accepted".
- **Use in the vault:** the charger-efficiency ranges are a regulator-draft statement of typical values by charger technology; they are not evidence of any product's efficiency. Compare with [[Metric - Peak Efficiency]].
- **Conflict:** Q3 2023 as the first metered-only report conflicts with Q4 2023 in the later DEQ guidance (C292).
- **Not stated:** whether DEQ approved or changed it, why the pre- and post-2016 split matters, and the basis of the 20 percent and two-cycle caps.
