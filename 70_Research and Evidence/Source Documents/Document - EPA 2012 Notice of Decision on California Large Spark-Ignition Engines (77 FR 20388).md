# Document - EPA 2012 Notice of Decision on California Large Spark-Ignition Engines (77 FR 20388)

## Definition

EPA's 2012 decision authorizing California's large spark-ignition nonroad engine program, with the Clean Air Act section 209(e) framework.

## Notes

**How it was read:** through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand), not the raw file.

Added for the regulatory-driver topic as the neutral federal source on why California needs EPA authorization for nonroad rules. EPA Notice of Decision, 77 FR 20388, published 2012-04-04 and signed 2012-03-29.

- **Framework:** federal law bars states from adopting or enforcing emission standards for new nonroad engines and also restricts regulation of non-new nonroad engines and vehicles; under Clean Air Act section 209(e)(2) EPA must authorize California to enforce its own nonroad standards after notice and hearing unless it makes one of three findings (the protectiveness determination is arbitrary and capricious; California does not need its own standards for compelling and extraordinary conditions; the standards are inconsistent with section 209). Opponents carry the burden of proof.
- **What this notice authorized:** California's large spark-ignition nonroad engine program: new-engine standards from model year 2007 (stricter from 2010), certification and test procedures, and in-use fleet-average requirements for large and medium fleets of equipment such as forklifts, sweepers and scrubbers, airport ground support equipment and industrial tow tractors (fleets with four or more LSI units). CARB submitted the request in December 2008.
- **Why it matters here:** CARB's 2025 notices say the newer ZEF regulation still needs a section 209(e)(2)(A) decision. Whether the 2012-authorized fleet-average rule still applies to private fleets today was not checked (C289).
