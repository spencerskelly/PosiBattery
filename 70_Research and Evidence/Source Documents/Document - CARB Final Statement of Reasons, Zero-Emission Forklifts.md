## Notes

**Content recorded:**
- About half of California's forklift population already uses zero-emission technology. Staff assumed one charger per zero-emission forklift and a lead-acid life of 1,500 recharging cycles; lithium has about twice the cycles and about 10% higher round-trip efficiency. Costs were amortized at 5% over five years and an inflation and supply-chain factor of 76% was applied to forklift costs.
- Typical fleet net savings about USD 6.0 million by 2043 (USD 5.6 million with doubled infrastructure cost; USD 5.1 million without LCFS credits); a small fleet an estimated net cost of USD 8,840 without credits; statewide cumulative savings USD 2.2bn without credits. A commenter claimed 8 to 16 hours of downtime for battery changes; staff disputed this for lithium-ion.

**Update 2026-10-18: file now in the repo and read first-hand** (Downloads/fsor.pdf, 2024 hearing date June 27, text searched for battery, charging and safety content):

- **Fire and life safety:** CARB staff state they are "not aware of additional fire or life safety requirements that pertain to lithium-ion battery charging", and that lead-acid may need additional fire and life safety equipment. This differs from the insurer guidance in [[Document - If P&C Hazard Info Sheet Lithium-Ion Stacker Trucks (2024-06)]] (C353).
- **Ventilation:** a commenter said most end users would need to upgrade space for venting; CARB agreed some lead-acid charging rooms may need changes but said minimal ventilation is needed for lithium and fuel-cell trucks.
- **Technology claims by staff:** lithium has about twice the cycles of flooded lead-acid and about 10 percent higher round-trip efficiency (150 charges lost over 1,500 lead-acid cycles); staff expect lead-acid to stay the dominant zero-emission technology in the near term because of capital cost and existing support systems; staff assumed one Level 2-class charger per truck.
- **Opportunity charging:** staff and a cited Green Cubes article say lithium suits opportunity charging at shift breaks, while lead-acid trucks rely on battery change or full charge between shifts.
