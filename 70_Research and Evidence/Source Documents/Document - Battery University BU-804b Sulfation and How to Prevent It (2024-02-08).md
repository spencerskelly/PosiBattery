# Document - Battery University BU-804b Sulfation and How to Prevent It (2024-02-08)

## Definition

Battery University page on lead-acid sulfation, its causes, prevention and recovery, including pulse devices.

## Notes

**How it was read:** through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand).

- Sulfation: small sulfate crystals are harmless in normal use; prolonged charge deprivation makes stable crystals on the negative plates. Two types named, reversible and permanent; permanent sets in after weeks or months at low state of charge.
- Prevention: a periodic full charge, said to take 14 to 16 hours to saturate; an 8-hour overnight charge is called not enough (examples are wheelchair, starter and off-grid batteries, not forklifts).
- Corrective step for reversible sulfation: about 200 mA to a fully charged battery, terminal voltage held at 2.50 to 2.66 V per cell for about 24 hours, with the battery warmed to 50 to 60 C (C342).
- Pulse or anti-sulfation devices: said to lower sulfation on a healthy battery but not reverse established sulfation; called unscientific and a possible cause of soft shorts, ripple heating and grid corrosion. No test data cited.
- The page itself says recovery yield is not fully understood and sulfation cannot be measured simply. Cites only other Battery University pages.
