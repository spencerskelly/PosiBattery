## Notes

**Content recorded (as stated by the source, not independently verified unless marked):**
- OSHA classes: I electric rider trucks, II electric narrow aisle trucks, III electric hand or hand/rider trucks; ANSI/ITSDF B56.1 is the safety standard. The class does not set battery voltage; the manufacturer and model do.
- Typical voltage and capacity as stated: Class III 24 V (36 V on some), 150 to 300 Ah; Class II 36 or 48 V, often 400 Ah and above; Class I 36, 48 or 80 V, 400 to 900+ Ah.
- Models stated: 24 V 230 Ah at 25.6 V nominal, 5.9 kWh, 115 A continuous and 175 A peak, 662 x 650 x 195 mm, about 105 kg. 36 V 920 Ah (no other specification). 48 V 460 Ah at 51.2 V, 23.6 kWh, 100 A continuous and 350 A peak, 32.7 x 22.0 x 16.0 in, about 507 lb, IP67, REMA 320 or Anderson 350. 80 V 460 Ah, 36.8 kWh, 100 A continuous and 350 A peak, 39.0 x 24.0 x 16.9 in, about 595 lb.
- Claims the 48 V 460 Ah is a direct swap into standard 48 V compartments and the 80 V 460 Ah a like-for-like replacement for 80 V lead-acid without structural modification (C180).
- Safety standards: truck under ANSI/ITSDF B56.1; pack generally evaluated against UL's standard for batteries used in electric vehicles. Cell supplier EVE, IP67, 10-year warranty (vendor claims).

**Checks made:**
- Energy figures equal Ah x nominal voltage (for example 460 x 51.2 = 23.55 kWh) and are called usable (C178). Pack mass implies 56, 102 and 136 Wh/kg across models (C179).
