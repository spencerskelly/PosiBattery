# Western State Clean Fuel Programs for Forklift Electricity Register

## Definition

Register of how Oregon's Clean Fuels Program, Washington's Clean Fuel Standard and New Mexico's Clean Transportation Fuel Program treat forklift charging electricity, compared with California. It records who reports, how energy must be measured, dates, source conflicts and what each means for chargers and their data. Evidence sits on the Document notes; disagreements are numbered C291 to C295 in [[Battery Product Landscape Conflicts and Open Questions]].

## Notes

- **Reading rule:** every source was read through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand). State agency publications sit outside the T1 to T5 tiers; consultancy and vendor sources are T4. "Analyst reasoning" marks a conclusion no source states. Status is as of 2026-10-17. California is covered in [[California Forklift Electrification Regulations Register]].
- **Scope:** how Oregon, Washington and New Mexico clean fuel programs treat forklift charging electricity (reporting entity, metering, registration, dates). Credit prices, EER values and rule text were not obtained (IB-253 to IB-257).

### Bottom line (analyst summary)

1. **Oregon requires direct metering and forbids adjusting the data.** Since the Q4 2023 report only directly metered forklifts generate credits, and metered data may not be adjusted except to subtract another party's claimed charging ([[Document - Oregon DEQ Forklift Transition Guidance (2023)]]). California instead multiplies upstream-metered kWh by charger efficiency, 85 percent by default ([[Document - CARB LCFS Guidance 25-01 Forklift Fueling Reporting (2025-10)]]). The two rules differ (C291).
2. **Oregon lets the charger itself be the registered equipment** when its data tracking is documented ([[Document - Oregon DEQ Forklift Transition Guidance (2023)]]). California's guidance is silent on charger-reported data (C287). This is the first evidence in the vault that a regulator accepts charger-level data.
3. **Washington names the fleet owner as the reporting entity and credit generator** for forklift charging (operator by written contract), but the rule section read has no metering requirement and the agency slides mention direct metering and non-metered methods (C293).
4. **New Mexico reporting began 2026-04-01.** The state's outreach document lists electric forklifts as special equipment and requires metered quarterly kWh; a consultancy summary does not mention forklifts, and the rule text was not read (C294).
5. **The reporting party differs by state** (C295), so a multi-state customer cannot assume one answer.

### Implications for chargers and data (analyst reasoning, hypotheses only)

- **H7, one measured basis.** Energy measured at the charger output or on the forklift, with no adjustment applied, would be usable under Oregon's no-adjustment rule and would need no California efficiency adjustment. This assumes the charger meter meets each program's accuracy expectations, which are not evidenced for any PosiCharge product (REQ-00058).
- **H8, record the measurement point.** Oregon registration depends on meter location and on documented data collection; a data record that names the measurement point and the charger would match what registration asks for (REQ-00058).
- **H9, rental forklifts.** Oregon and California both require attention to which party's charging is claimed (double counting), which suggests per-forklift identity matters for rental fleets. Not evidenced for any product.
- **H10, sizing.** None of the sources give credit prices or revenue per forklift for these states; New Mexico's $100 per credit for 2027 is a projection in the state's own document. No business case can be drawn yet (IB-256).
