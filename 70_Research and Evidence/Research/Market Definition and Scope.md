# Market Definition and Scope

## Definition

What "the market" means for battery-adjacent equipment on industrial trucks, which public statistics measure which part, and what each region's statistics cover. Written 2026-10-18 from public sources only, to sit beside [[Market Overview]], [[Market Size and Volume Register]] and [[Regional Market Size Model]]. Where a figure is already in those notes it is not repeated.

## Notes

### 1. The market is a chain, and each link has its own statistics

| Layer | What is sold | Best public measure in the vault | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trucks (the host) | New industrial trucks, Classes 1 to 5 | World Industrial Truck Statistics (WITS): 2,381,417 shipments in 2025 ([[Market Size and Volume Register]]) | Units only; no price; no stock |
| Traction batteries | Lead-acid and lithium packs, first fit plus replacement | Vendor reports USD 5.4 to 5.9bn; bottom-up model USD 12.2bn base ([[Battery Market Bottom-Up Model]]) | Vendor method unpublished; model inputs partly assumed |
| Chargers | Chargers sold with trucks or batteries and as replacements | Vendor reports USD 1.28 to 2.03bn (conflict W4) | Two vendors differ by 59% |
| Monitors, BMIDs, connected services | Hardware plus software on the battery, charger or truck | None found | No public unit or revenue statistic (see [[Charger and Monitor Public Price Register]]) |
| Energy and credits | Clean fuel credits, rebates, demand savings | Program data in [[North American Clean Fuel Programs Register]] | Value depends on policy, not equipment sales |

Each layer lies downstream of the one above it. Truck shipments are the only layer with global unit data; everything below is derived or vendor-sized.

### 2. What the statistics count

| Source | Population counted | Classes | Regions | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WITS (FEM, ITA, JIVA, CITA and others) | Industrial trucks reported by participating manufacturers | 1, 2, 31, 32, 4/5 | Europe, Americas, Asia, Africa, Oceania; no country table | Used throughout the vault. Americas = North plus South America |
| ITA factory shipments | US factory shipments of ITA members since 1983 | Electric rider (1+2 combined), motorized hand (3), internal combustion (4+5 combined) | United States | ITA represents 85% of forklift manufacturers in the US and Canada; the page warns annual figures are not comparable because membership changes ([[Document - ITA Market Intelligence page]]). Table read first-hand 2026-10-18 (2000 to 2025; see [[Regional Market Size Model]] Table E) |
| ITA retail orders | North American retail orders | 1 to 5 | North America | 344,330 in 2022; electric about 65% in 2022 ([[Market Size and Volume Register]]) |
| OSHA / ASME B56.1 | Definition of a powered industrial truck for US rules | all | United States | Mobile, power-propelled trucks used to carry, push, pull, lift, stack or tier material; about 1.5 million operators ([[Document - OSHA Directive CPL 18-07 Powered Industrial Truck Enforcement Region IV (2017)]]) |
| NAICS 333924 | US establishments making industrial trucks, tractors, trailers and stackers | n/a | United States | Industry code, not a product count; vendor TAM of about USD 38bn has no stated geography ([[Document - Grata NAICS 333924 Industrial Truck Tractor Trailer Stacker Machinery Manufacturing Market Overview]]) |
| CCMA (China) | Chinese forklift sales, domestic plus export | not stated | China | 1,285,500 in 2024; exceeds WITS Asia (C355) |

**Class names (as labelled in [[Market Size and Volume Register]]).** Class 1 electric rider counterbalanced; Class 2 electric warehouse rider; Class 31 electric pedestrian pallet (up to 250 kg); Class 32 other electric pedestrian; Classes 4 and 5 internal combustion. ITA's published groupings merge Class 1 with 2 and Class 4 with 5, so WITS and ITA cannot be compared class by class.

**Reading the Brazil density ratio.** If Brazil's rate was 8 per 100,000 and Germany's 130, Brazil sells about one sixteenth as many machines per head as Germany (derived). The article does not say whether the count includes hand pallet trucks or rentals. Applying the ratio to Brazil's population would give a unit estimate, but no population figure is in the vault and the basis of the ratio is a manufacturer's remark, so no unit estimate is made here.

### 4. What is still not defined

1. **Canada and Mexico units.** The ITA US factory shipments table and the North American retail-orders slide are now read (2026-10-18, files supplied from the public ITA site); they give the US and a combined North America, not Canada or Mexico separately. FEM, CITA, JIVA and the WITS sheet PDFs are still wanted (R78).
2. **Installed base by region, class and age.** Section 5 lists what exists: one US figure from 2009, one manufacturer's own base, and a derived range. A March 2019 trade article says OSHA asked for a fleet census but gave no numbers. Nothing was found for Europe, Asia, Mexico or Brazil.
3. **A unit definition shared across regions.** WITS, ITA and CCMA counts differ, so regional unit totals from different sources should not be added.
4. **Rental and used trucks.** About 12% of North American retail shipments went to rental in 2017 (one manufacturer's 10-K); nothing found for other regions beyond Brazilian dealer remarks, and no used-truck data.
5. **Addressable subset for chargers.** Which trucks use opportunity or fast charging, and at what voltage and power, is not defined by any public statistic ([[Regional Market Size Model]] uses Class 1 plus 2 as a proxy).

### 5. Installed base: what public sources say (added 2026-10-18)

| Statement | Scope | Year | Source (tier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Over one million lift trucks in operation, in more than 900 industries | Hyster-Yale's own trucks, worldwide | end of 2023 | [[Document - Hyster-Yale 2023 Annual Report Narrative (installed base statement)]] (T1) |
| Over 1 million forklifts in operation in the US; about 2 million operators | US, all makers | 2009 | [[Document - NIST PerMIS 2009 Forklift Safety White Paper]] (T3, citing a 2009 SBIR report) |
| About 1.5 million workers operate powered industrial trucks | US operators | 2017 | [[Document - OSHA Directive CPL 18-07 Powered Industrial Truck Enforcement Region IV (2017)]] (T1) |
| Stock of roughly 2 to 3.5 million trucks | US; derived from sales times a 10-year life assumed, not sourced | n/a | [[Fleet Energy and Cost Evidence Register]] [A] |
| About 12% of North American retail shipments went to rental | North America | 2017 | [[Document - Hyster-Yale Form 10-K for Fiscal 2017 (installed base, aftermarket and rental statements)]] (T1) |
| Aftermarket parts about 13% of Hyster-Yale revenue | One manufacturer | 2015 to 2017 | same |

**Reading these together (C359).** The only sourced US stock figure is about 1 million (2009, secondary), against 2 to 3.5 million from sales times an assumed life. The 2009 figure may count only trucks in regular use, or may be wrong; the derived range rests on an unsourced life. ITA US factory shipments of 222,449 to 298,059 a year in 2019 to 2025 ([[Document - ITA United States Factory Shipments Table (2000 to 2025, 2026 Directory)]]) fit the 2 to 3.5 million range only if the truck life is about 10 years. Hyster-Yale's own installed base of over one million is a share of the market, not the market. None of these gives stock by class, age or region. A stock-from-shipments model for the US is in [[US Installed Base Model]] (about 1.9 million at an 8-year life, 1.2 to 2.7 million for 5 to 12 years).
