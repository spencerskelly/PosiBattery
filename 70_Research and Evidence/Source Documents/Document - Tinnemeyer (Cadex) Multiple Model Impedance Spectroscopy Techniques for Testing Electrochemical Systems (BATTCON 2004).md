# Document - Tinnemeyer (Cadex) Multiple Model Impedance Spectroscopy Techniques for Testing Electrochemical Systems (BATTCON 2004)

## Definition

Cadex author paper (vendor-authored) on the limits of single-frequency ohmic testing and the Spectro multi-frequency, multi-model alternative.

## Notes

- Vendor-authored: the paper presents the Cadex solution. The paper states no year; 2004 comes from the file path and the author's file name (TinnemeyerPaper2004).
- Claims: single-frequency commercial testers use below 100 Hz, typically 83 to 90 Hz; the paper says accurate assessment of large stationary batteries needs below 20 Hz, and that precision exists only for batteries at 0 to 75 percent capacity, citing its reference 10.
- Spectro as described: 20 to 2000 Hz, voltage-controlled (potentiostatic) excitation regulated to 10 mV across each cell, accuracy of plus or minus 250 micro-ohm across the spectrum, excitation isolated from the dc voltage so a battery can be tested while charging or discharging. Fits Randles-type models by Levenberg-Marquardt.
- States that IEEE 450 guidelines demand replacement of batteries below 80 percent capacity. This is the statement behind C349; the standard itself is unread.
- Not stated: tested cell sizes or voltages, accuracy against a capacity reference, and any traction use.
