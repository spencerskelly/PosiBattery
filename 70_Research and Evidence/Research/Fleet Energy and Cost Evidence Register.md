# Fleet Energy and Cost Evidence Register

## Definition

Public evidence on how many industrial trucks are in service, how much energy they and their buildings use, what batteries they carry, and what they cost to own, seen from different points of view (regulator, lead-battery industry, propane industry, lithium and OEM vendors, utilities, national laboratories, federal statistics). Built 2026-10-18. Feeds the fleet side of [[Battery Market Bottom-Up Model]]. All data is from public sources; every source was read through a summarizing fetch tool.

## Notes

### 1. Points of view

### 5. What this changes in the bottom-up model

- **Class 3 batteries are not small.** Field data put a Class III pallet jack battery at 12.24 kWh against 42.8 to 48.0 kWh for Class I and II (derived ratio about 0.26 to 0.29). The model's Class 3 price (USD 500 to 1,500) is an unsourced assumption and the energy data give no price; leave it flagged.
- **Lead-acid packs are larger than lithium for the same truck.** Class I lead-acid at 42.84 kWh against lithium Class 1 packs of 22.7 to 33.6 kWh (derived: lithium is 53 to 78% of the lead-acid size) because lead-acid is sized for about 80% depth of discharge. Price per truck should be compared, not price per kWh.
- **Batteries per truck.** CARB assumes one charger per zero-emission forklift, which supports one battery per truck for lithium; multi-shift lead-acid needs spares (sponsored source: two to three).
- **Fleet check.** California's 70,000 lead-acid zero-emission forklifts at a 4-year battery life would need about 17,500 replacement batteries a year (derived; life assumed, one battery per truck); the state is one data point for scaling and is not enough to set a US stock.
