# PosiCharge Market Segments and Jobs-to-Be-Done

## Definition

Define the operational contexts and customer jobs used to judge relevance, product comparability, partnerships, and gaps for PosiCharge.

## Notes

### Initial market segments

| Segment | Operating context | Primary energy challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material-handling fleets | Warehouses, distribution centers, manufacturing, and multi-shift operations | Fleet availability, battery life, labor, charge throughput, and safety |
| Airport eGSE fleets | Outdoor, mission-critical ground-support operations | Fast turnaround, outdoor durability, availability, and fleet visibility |
| Mixed-chemistry fleets | Lead-acid and lithium fleets, often during transition | Correct charging behavior, identification, safety, and operational consistency |
| Battery-room and centralized charging operations | Fixed charging locations with many assets and operators | Utilization, queueing, energy operations, battery handling, maintenance, and accountability |
| Distributed and opportunity charging operations | Chargers located near work areas or used during work breaks | Charge opportunity, infrastructure coordination, uptime, and operator workflow |

### Initial customer jobs

- Keep revenue-producing electric equipment available throughout the required shift or mission.
- Charge each battery safely and appropriately for its chemistry, state, and use history.
- Reduce battery damage, premature replacement, avoidable maintenance, and unsafe events.
- Know which battery is connected, where it is used, and how charging behavior affects fleet performance.
- Manage charger, battery, and fleet energy data without creating excessive operator or maintenance burden.
- Deploy and service equipment in the actual operating environment, including outdoor and airport conditions where relevant.
- Integrate energy systems with the fleet, battery, vehicle, and software ecosystem where value is demonstrated.

### Segmentation rules

- Do not treat all industrial fleets as a single segment.
- Do not infer a job or requirement from an organization type alone.
- Treat chemistry, duty cycle, operating environment, electrical infrastructure, fleet size, and service model as comparison variables.
- Link each product comparison and gap statement to at least one segment and job.
