# Cost Driver 10: Workforce Hiring, Training, and Retention

## Executive summary

Warehouse and production facilities incur workforce costs beyond direct hourly labor: recruiting, agency markups, vacancy coverage, overtime, onboarding, trainer time, reduced output during ramp-up, certification administration, early turnover, absenteeism, and quality or safety losses caused by inexperienced personnel. This report examines products already used by businesses to reduce those costs, focusing on workforce-management systems, on-demand labor marketplaces, retention analytics, frontline learning platforms, and forklift simulation.

The strongest commercial pattern is an integrated workforce loop: forecast workload, schedule the right skills, fill gaps quickly, train and certify workers, measure readiness and performance, collect feedback, and intervene before workers leave. Existing products address separate parts of this loop, but integration among workforce, training, operational, safety, and MHE-energy data remains incomplete.

Published results are predominantly vendor-authored case studies rather than controlled studies. They demonstrate commercial use and plausible value, but each claimed result should be validated in a site-specific pilot with baseline definitions, control periods, and finance-approved savings rules.

## Cost-driver definition

This cost driver includes:

- Recruiting advertising, recruiters, staffing agencies, screening, background checks, and hiring administration.
- Unfilled-shift losses, supervisor expediting, overtime, temporary-labor premiums, and missed production or shipment commitments.
- Orientation, classroom instruction, equipment training, practical evaluation, mentoring, and reduced productivity during ramp-up.
- Certification, refresher training, skills records, expiration tracking, and compliance administration.
- Early attrition, repeat recruiting and onboarding, schedule instability, absenteeism, burnout, and loss of experienced operators.
- Errors, damage, incidents, and downtime attributable to inadequate knowledge or insufficient task and equipment experience.

For powered industrial trucks, OSHA requires formal and practical training, workplace evaluation, employer certification, refresher training under specified conditions, and operator evaluation at least every three years. Simulation or e-learning can support formal instruction and practice, but it does not replace required hands-on training and workplace evaluation.[^1][^2]

## Products in use today

### Labor planning and management

#### Blue Yonder Workforce and Labor Management

Blue Yonder combines labor forecasting, scheduling, skills and availability, mobile workforce tools, and real-time operational signals. DSC Logistics reported a greater than 20% reduction in variable labor spending after implementing labor management across its network; the system supported standardized work methods, productivity measurement, workforce forecasting, and demand-based scheduling.[^3][^4]

**Needs solved**

- Poor alignment between volume and scheduled labor.
- Inconsistent labor standards and productivity measurement.
- Excess overtime, idle time, and reactive reassignment.
- Limited visibility into skill availability by shift and area.

**Productivity gained**

- Faster staffing-plan creation.
- Intraday movement of labor toward constrained work areas.
- More consistent measurement of expected versus actual work.
- Better use of employee skills and availability.

**Costs reduced**

- Variable labor spend.
- Overtime and overstaffing.
- Supervisor planning and reporting time.
- Service failures resulting from inadequate coverage.

**Valuable options**

- Skill and certification constraints in the scheduling optimizer.
- Employee preference and availability capture.
- WMS, WES, time-clock and payroll integration.
- Separate labor standards for manual, MHE-assisted and automated workflows.
- Intraday alerts that account for equipment and battery availability.

#### UKG workforce management

UKG products automate time capture, scheduling, leave and pay-rule processing. DHL Supply Chain India used UKG Workforce Central to replace manual attendance, leave, overtime, compliance and contract-labor tracking; reported outcomes included reduced administrative effort, better invoice accuracy, lower overpayment and compliance risk, and improved process efficiency across locations.[^5]

Marley Spoon used UKG Ready and InTouch clocks to consolidate payroll data, automate complex pay rules, simplify warehouse location clock-ins, and reduce payroll processing from days to hours.[^6]

**Needs solved**

- Fragmented attendance and payroll records.
- Manual interpretation of work and pay rules.
- Weak visibility into contract labor and multi-location time.
- Payroll errors and time-consuming exception handling.

**Productivity gained**

- Faster clock-in and payroll workflows.
- Real-time labor visibility for supervisors.
- Automated exception alerts and rule application.
- Less administrative work per employee and shift.

**Costs reduced**

- Payroll errors, overpayments and time theft.
- HR and supervisor administration.
- Compliance exposure.
- Contract-labor invoice discrepancies.

**Valuable options**

- Geofenced or station-specific time capture.
- Direct connection to WMS labor activities.
- Credential checks before accepting a shift or truck assignment.
- Fatigue and overtime rules that prevent unsafe assignments.
- Contractor invoice reconciliation against verified work events.

### Flexible staffing platforms

#### Veryable

Veryable provides an on-demand labor marketplace and workforce-management platform for manufacturing, warehousing and logistics. A regional 3PL case study reported 44% higher efficiency and a 40.9% reduction in cost per unit after using on-demand operators with workforce management; the vendor attributed $49,000 in marketplace savings and $53,500 in WFM savings over the reported periods.[^7]

The platform is also used to build reusable pools of workers familiar with a facility. Courier Express used preferred-worker functionality to scale by 20–30 operators per week during peaks while reducing the repeated retraining associated with revolving temporary workers.[^8]

**Needs solved**

- Volatile demand and short-notice labor shortages.
- Scarcity of experienced forklift and order-picker operators.
- High fixed headcount or overtime used to cover peaks.
- Inconsistent temporary-worker quality.

**Productivity gained**

- Faster filling of open shifts.
- Flexible capacity scaled to daily demand.
- Reuse of workers already familiar with the site.
- Better visibility into worker skills and prior performance.

**Costs reduced**

- Overtime and excess permanent staffing.
- Backlog, detention and lost throughput.
- Repeated orientation and retraining.
- Cost per unit during demand swings.

**Valuable options**

- Verified PIT class, attachment and site qualifications.
- Integration with access control and truck login.
- Site-specific micro-orientation before shift acceptance.
- Worker performance scorecards using quality, safety and attendance—not speed alone.
- Preferred pools segmented by department, truck type and shift.

#### Instawork

Instawork is a flexible staffing platform serving warehouse, logistics and manufacturing operations. Goo Goo Cluster used it to create a 25-worker roster, fill 96% of open shifts and increase wholesale shipping by 300% during seasonal demand; the product also streamlined scheduling and time tracking.[^9]

**Needs solved**

- Seasonal or promotional staffing spikes.
- Slow response from conventional staffing providers.
- Manual shift scheduling and timekeeping.
- Lack of a reusable contingent-worker bench.

**Productivity gained**

- Faster staffing and improved fulfillment capacity.
- Rapid scaling up or down with shipment volume.
- Simplified shift and attendance administration.

**Costs reduced**

- Lost sales or shipments caused by insufficient staffing.
- Internal scheduling effort.
- Fixed labor carried outside peak periods.
- Delays and premium freight caused by backlog.

**Valuable options**

- Pre-shift digital work instructions and knowledge checks.
- Credential expiration enforcement.
- Integration with LMS, WMS, payroll and access control.
- Site familiarity and repeat-worker scoring.
- Automated comparison of contingent, overtime and permanent-labor economics.

### Retention and feedback analytics

#### WorkStep

WorkStep provides recruiting and retention analytics for supply-chain frontline roles. A Fortune 100 food-and-beverage company used its Retain platform to segment employee feedback and attrition by region, role and facility; the case study reports a 29% reduction in new-hire turnover and $1.4 million in cost-of-turnover savings in the second quarter of the partnership.[^10]

A multinational 3PL used WorkStep for warehouse associate, material-handler and forklift-operator recruiting. The case study reports greater than 85% retention for the first 100 hires, 50 offers in one week at one location, and expansion to eight facilities in two months.[^11]

**Needs solved**

- Poor visibility into why workers leave.
- Retention variation across sites, managers and roles.
- Slow warehouse and forklift-operator recruiting.
- Actions based on anecdote rather than segmented feedback.

**Productivity gained**

- Faster identification of retention drivers.
- More focused interventions by site and role.
- Better continuity of experienced teams.
- Reduced supervisory disruption caused by constant replacement hiring.

**Costs reduced**

- Recruiting and replacement onboarding.
- Vacancy overtime and agency use.
- Productivity loss during repeated ramp-up.
- Quality and safety variance from inexperienced labor.

**Valuable options**

- Join/leave data from HRIS with WMS performance and safety events.
- Privacy-preserving manager and shift analysis.
- Early-tenure check-ins triggered at 7, 30, 60 and 90 days.
- Closed-loop intervention tracking with accountable owners.
- Exit-risk analysis that excludes protected traits and is audited for bias.

### Frontline learning platforms

#### Axonify

Axonify delivers short, personalized learning and communications to frontline workers. Its logistics materials report that Wakefern cut new-hire training time by 50% and increased knowledge scores by 31%; Walmart distribution-center deployments reportedly increased safety knowledge by as much as 15% and reduced recordable incidents by 54% at eight sites.[^12]

**Needs solved**

- Long classroom-based onboarding.
- Inconsistent SOP knowledge across shifts and sites.
- Knowledge decay after initial training.
- Difficulty distributing process, safety and system changes quickly.

**Productivity gained**

- Faster progression to independent work.
- Training delivered in small intervals without removing teams for long sessions.
- Targeted remediation based on individual knowledge gaps.
- Consistent rollout of new WMS, equipment and customer procedures.

**Costs reduced**

- Instructor and classroom time.
- New-hire ramp cost.
- Mis-picks, rework, shrink and incidents.
- Administrative effort for training assignment and completion tracking.

**Valuable options**

- Role-, equipment- and location-specific learning paths.
- Multilingual content and literacy-appropriate media.
- Training triggered by impact, near-miss or quality events.
- Just-in-time job aids at charger, truck or workstation.
- Knowledge checks linked to authorization without representing e-learning as complete PIT certification.

### Forklift simulation

#### Raymond Virtual Reality Simulator

Raymond commercialized a VR forklift simulator that uses actual truck controls and expanded across multiple Raymond models and lessons. The system is used for novice training and workforce-development programs, allowing practice before live equipment operation.[^13]

#### Forklift-Simulator and Virtual Forklift

Forklift-Simulator reports deployments with more than 200 companies and organizations, including DHL, Ryder, BMW, PepsiCo and the U.S. Air Force. An Employbridge/ProLogistix deployment reportedly reduced average training from 45 to 12 hours for more than 5,000 learners, while a Ryder user reported doubling simultaneous practice capacity.[^14][^15]

A warehouse and distribution case study from SkillsVR reports that a hardware retailer reduced average forklift-training time from several hours to under one hour for the simulated portion and rolled the program out after a successful pilot. The simulator standardized scenarios and tracked hazard recognition, visual focus and safety routines.[^16]

**Needs solved**

- Limited live trucks, safe training areas and qualified trainer capacity.
- Risk of equipment, rack, product or facility damage during early practice.
- Inconsistent scenarios and subjective readiness decisions.
- Need to expose operators to rare hazards without real-world danger.

**Productivity gained**

- Parallel practice while production trucks remain in service.
- Faster initial familiarization and screening.
- Repeatable scenarios and objective performance data.
- Training outside operating areas and production hours.

**Costs reduced**

- Live-truck and trainer time.
- Training damage and incident exposure.
- Production disruption and dedicated training-space requirements.
- Cost of discovering poor aptitude late in onboarding.

**Important limitation**

VR is a supplement, not a complete OSHA PIT qualification. The employer still needs practical training under direct supervision and evaluation of performance in the actual workplace.[^17][^1]

**Valuable options**

- Actual controls for each truck class and attachment.
- Site digital twins reproducing aisles, intersections, docks and battery rooms.
- Eye tracking, head checks, pedestrian recognition and stability scoring.
- Scenario libraries for charging, battery exchange, opportunity charging and connector handling.
- Automatic LMS records and individualized practical-training plans.
- Post-incident replay and targeted refresher modules.

## Productivity and cost model

The business case should separate capacity improvement from cashable savings. Faster onboarding is not automatically a financial saving unless it reduces paid training hours, trainer utilization, vacancies, temporary labor, overtime, errors, incidents, or required headcount.

### Core measures

| Measure | Calculation | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Time to proficiency | Days from hire to sustained target output and quality | Primary ramp-up KPI |
| Ramp labor cost | Trainee paid hours + trainer hours + reduced-output cost | Full onboarding burden |
| Early attrition | Leavers within 30, 60 and 90 days / hires | Indicates hiring and onboarding effectiveness |
| Cost per retained hire | Recruiting and onboarding cost / hires remaining after chosen period | Better than cost per hire alone |
| Shift fill rate | Filled qualified shifts / requested shifts | Staffing reliability |
| Overtime dependency | Overtime hours / total labor hours | Capacity mismatch and burnout indicator |
| Qualified coverage | Qualified operators available / operators required by truck class and shift | Measures operational resilience |
| Trainer leverage | Learner practice hours / trainer hours | Captures simulator and digital-learning scalability |
| First-pass quality | Correct transactions or units / total units | Detects training-related errors |
| Safety during ramp | Incidents and near misses per new-hire hours | Measures readiness without rewarding unsafe speed |

### Savings equations

Annualized turnover saving:

\[
(\text{baseline exits} - \text{post-deployment exits}) \times \text{validated replacement cost per exit}
\]

Onboarding saving:

\[
(\text{baseline training hours} - \text{new training hours}) \times (\text{trainee rate} + \text{allocated trainer rate})
\]

Capacity value from faster ramp:

\[
\text{productive hours gained} \times \text{validated contribution per productive hour}
\]

Flexible-labor saving:

\[
\text{avoided overtime} + \text{avoided idle permanent labor} + \text{avoided backlog cost} - \text{platform and contingent-labor premium}
\]

Only amounts that change a budget, avoid an approved expenditure, or create demonstrable incremental margin should be classified as cashable savings.

## MHE and energy adjacencies

A connected MHE-energy platform can extend beyond charger and battery monitoring by becoming a trusted source of equipment readiness, operator authorization and task-specific learning signals.

| Product extension | Product function | Customer need solved | Value delivered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operator qualification service | Maintain operator-to-truck-class, attachment, site and expiration matrix | Supervisors cannot easily confirm who may operate what | Faster assignments, fewer unauthorized starts, lower compliance administration |
| Truck access interlock | Permit login only when role and qualification rules are satisfied | Training records and truck access are disconnected | Enforced authorization and auditable control |
| Skill-aware shift readiness | Combine scheduled demand, qualified operators, trucks, batteries and chargers | A nominal headcount may not provide required operational capability | Earlier detection of capacity gaps and reduced shift-start delay |
| Event-triggered coaching | Trigger microlearning after impact, charger misuse, low-voltage event or repeated procedural exception | Generic annual training does not address observed behavior | Targeted correction with less classroom time |
| Battery-room training module | Deliver site-specific workflows for charging, connector safety, watering and battery exchange | Battery procedures are safety-critical and inconsistently executed | Reduced battery damage, connector damage, delays and unsafe handling |
| Digital skill passport | Portable internal record of truck, energy-system and site competencies | Multi-site transfers require repeated record collection | Faster deployment while preserving site-specific evaluation requirements |
| New-hire ramp dashboard | Track qualification, productive hours, impacts, energy-use behavior and quality milestones | Managers lack a unified readiness view | Earlier intervention and evidence-based release to independent work |
| Trainer-capacity planner | Schedule instructors, simulators, trucks and safe areas against hiring plans | Trainer and equipment bottlenecks delay onboarding | Higher training throughput and less production-equipment disruption |
| Contractor orientation gateway | Require site induction and knowledge checks before temporary-worker access | Flexible labor arrives faster than conventional onboarding can support | Rapid but controlled contingent-labor deployment |
| Skills API | Exchange qualification and readiness data with HRIS, LMS, WMS, WES and access control | Records are duplicated across systems | Lower administration and fewer stale credentials |

## Recommended product architecture

### Data entities

- Person, employer and worker type.
- Site, department, zone and shift.
- Role, skill, truck class and attachment.
- Course, practical evaluation, evaluator and expiration.
- Truck, battery, charger and operational status.
- Work event, impact, safety event, quality exception and coaching action.
- Authorization decision and reason code.

### Integrations

- HRIS and workforce management for worker status, schedules and time.
- LMS for learning assignments, completion and assessment.
- WMS/WES for task demand, productivity and quality.
- Telematics and access control for operator login and truck events.
- Battery and charger platforms for equipment readiness and procedural exceptions.
- CMMS/EAM for damage or maintenance events linked to training needs.

### Design principles

- Keep safety authorization separate from productivity scoring.
- Support offline enforcement when cloud connectivity is lost.
- Record the source, effective date and expiration of every credential.
- Require site-specific practical evaluation where applicable.
- Minimize collection of personal data and apply role-based access.
- Audit algorithms for disparate impact and prohibit protected-characteristic inference.
- Explain every denied authorization or recommended intervention.

## Implementation roadmap

### Phase 1: Visibility

- Create a unified operator-skill and certification matrix.
- Import training records and identify expirations or gaps.
- Add shift-level qualified-coverage reporting.
- Measure baseline ramp time, early attrition, overtime, training hours and incidents.

### Phase 2: Workflow

- Automate expiration notifications and training assignments.
- Add truck and charger QR/NFC job aids.
- Trigger coaching from defined safety, energy and quality events.
- Integrate training status with workforce scheduling.

### Phase 3: Control

- Enforce operator-to-truck authorization.
- Prevent assignment where required skills or evaluations are missing.
- Combine labor, truck, battery and charger readiness for shift planning.
- Add offline policy caching and auditable exception handling.

### Phase 4: Optimization

- Forecast skill demand from production and WMS volume.
- Recommend cross-training based on future coverage gaps.
- Optimize instructor, simulator and equipment allocation.
- Compare permanent, overtime and flexible-labor scenarios by service and cost.

## Pilot design

A useful pilot should include one facility, two comparable departments or shifts, and at least one complete hiring cohort. The pilot should run long enough to measure early retention and proficiency rather than only course completion.

### Baseline data

- Applicants, offers, acceptances and start dates.
- Shift requests, fills, no-shows and overtime.
- Orientation, classroom, simulator, practical and trainer hours.
- Days and hours to independent work.
- Output, quality and safety during the first 30, 60 and 90 days.
- Voluntary and involuntary attrition with standardized reason codes.
- Agency, recruiting, training and replacement costs.

### Acceptance criteria

- Reduction in median time to proficiency without degraded quality or safety.
- Reduction in trainer hours per qualified operator.
- Higher qualified shift-fill rate.
- Lower 30-, 60- or 90-day attrition.
- Lower overtime or temporary-labor dependence.
- Complete and auditable certification records.
- Positive operator and supervisor adoption without unacceptable administrative burden.

## Risks and conflicts

| Risk or conflict | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Speed versus safety | Productivity incentives may encourage unsafe operation | Gate productivity metrics with safety and quality thresholds |
| Simulation versus legal qualification | Buyers may assume VR alone certifies operators | Clearly separate formal learning, simulated practice, practical training and workplace evaluation |
| Worker surveillance concerns | Detailed performance data may reduce trust and retention | Data minimization, transparency, role-based access and worker feedback channels |
| Algorithmic bias | Hiring, scheduling or attrition models may create disparate impacts | Human review, bias testing, explainability and prohibited-feature controls |
| Vendor-authored ROI | Published outcomes may not transfer to another site | Baseline-controlled pilot and finance-approved savings validation |
| Credential portability | Prior training may not match truck or workplace conditions | Validate equivalence and conduct site-specific evaluation |
| Contingent labor consistency | Fast staffing can increase variability and training burden | Preferred pools, verified skills, micro-orientation and controlled authorization |
| Integration failure | Stale HR or LMS data can wrongly authorize or block workers | Event monitoring, freshness indicators, fail-safe policies and exception workflows |
| Labor relations | Individual performance monitoring may conflict with agreements or culture | Engage HR, legal and labor stakeholders before deployment |

## Procurement criteria

- Demonstrated use in warehouses, production or logistics—not only retail or office environments.
- Verifiable customer references and clearly defined outcomes.
- APIs, bulk export, event webhooks and customer ownership of operational data.
- Support for contingent workers, multiple employers and multi-site operations.
- Skill, truck-class, attachment and site-specific qualification records.
- Mobile and offline operation on shared devices.
- Multilingual and accessible learning content.
- Configurable retention periods, privacy controls and audit logs.
- Integration with identity, HRIS, LMS, WMS, telematics, payroll and access control.
- Transparent pricing that includes implementation, content creation, devices, support and integrations.

## Product opportunity assessment

| Opportunity | Customer value | Strategic adjacency | Technical difficulty | Priority |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Unified operator qualification matrix | High | High | Low–medium | 1 |
| Skill-aware MHE shift-readiness dashboard | High | Very high | Medium | 2 |
| Event-triggered microlearning and coaching | High | Very high | Medium | 3 |
| Battery-room and charging competency module | Medium–high | Very high | Low–medium | 4 |
| Truck authorization integration | High | High | Medium–high | 5 |
| Trainer and simulator scheduling | Medium | Medium | Medium | 6 |
| Flexible-labor marketplace | High but outside core | Low | Very high | Partner rather than build |
| Full HRIS/payroll workforce suite | Broad but crowded | Low | Very high | Integrate rather than build |
| General-purpose LMS | Moderate but crowded | Medium | High | Partner; build MHE-specific content and triggers |

The best near-term product position is not a new staffing marketplace or general HR platform. It is an **MHE workforce-readiness layer** connecting operator qualifications, site-specific training, truck access, battery/charger readiness, shift demand and event-triggered coaching. This uses data and control points already adjacent to connected chargers, batteries and mobile equipment while complementing incumbent HR, LMS and WMS products.

## Future research

- Validate replacement-cost assumptions by role, site, tenure band and local labor market.
- Compare permanent, agency, marketplace and overtime labor using fully burdened cost per productive hour.
- Interview safety managers about requirements for electronic operator-qualification records and access interlocks.
- Quantify trainer capacity and production-equipment downtime consumed by current PIT onboarding.
- Test whether charger and battery events reliably identify coachable operator behaviors.
- Evaluate privacy and labor-law constraints for individual performance and retention analytics by jurisdiction.
- Benchmark API and data-access capabilities of UKG, Blue Yonder, Axonify, WorkStep, Veryable, Instawork and major forklift telematics providers.
- Assess partner-versus-build options for VR content, LMS delivery and identity/credential management.
- Develop a finance-approved pilot model tying ramp time, retention, overtime, safety and MHE availability to cashable savings.
