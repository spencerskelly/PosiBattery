# Document - MHI Blog LCFS Fuel Producer Credits for Material Handling (2025-10-28)

## Definition

MHI blog post explaining the LCFS changes for warehouses that own chargers, including metering from 2026.

## Notes

**How it was read:** through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand), not the raw file.

Added for the regulatory-driver topic. MHI blog post dated 2025-10-28, byline "Warehouse Automation Blog", quoting Oliver Stanbury of Stanbury Electrical Engineering (an MHI member, so a vendor voice).

- **Claims (author and the quoted member):** a warehouse that owns battery chargers counts as a fuel producer because the chargers convert AC to DC for electric forklifts; such operators can earn credits and sell them to high-carbon fuel producers; the carbon intensity target was cut by 20 percent, effective immediately at publication; from 2026-01-01 companies must meter and report electricity use before generating credits, where estimation models were used before; newer lithium batteries generally include monitoring systems, so the change should be manageable for those fleets, while older fleets may need equipment upgrades.
- **Other:** New Mexico's clean fuel regulations are said to take effect by 2026-07-01 (not checked). The post does not mention an energy economy ratio.
- **Agreement:** the metering date matches CARB's guidance ([[Document - CARB LCFS Guidance 25-01 Forklift Fueling Reporting (2025-10)]]); the claim that lithium battery monitoring is enough is the member's opinion and the guidance does not say whether battery-side data qualifies.
