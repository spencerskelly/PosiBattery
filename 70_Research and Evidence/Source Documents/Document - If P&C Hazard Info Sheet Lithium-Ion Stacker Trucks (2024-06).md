# Document - If P&C Hazard Info Sheet Lithium-Ion Stacker Trucks (2024-06)

## Definition

An insurer's loss-prevention sheet on lithium-ion stacker trucks: fire risks, where and how to charge, charger and BMS expectations. The most charger-relevant lithium-specific source found in this pass.

## Notes

**How it was read:** through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand), not the raw PDF (a direct link exists; see [[Document Wishlist]]). Read once; not cross-checked with a second prompt.

- **Risk statements:** heavy-duty trucks carry large packs; fires in them are hard to put out and can burn for hours or days; failing batteries can give off flammable gas (vapor cloud explosion risk) and toxic hydrogen fluoride; hydraulic oil adds to the fire load; the truck may not start a fire but can greatly feed one.
- **Charging location:** indoor charging is generally not recommended; if needed, a non-combustible building at least 10 m from surrounding buildings and storage. For an attached building: EI60 fire separation, automatic fire detection and sprinklers, and chargers that shut off when the fire alarm sounds. A 10 m safety distance applies even to open or canopy charging areas. Place stations on an external wall reachable from outside, ideally near an exit so a burning truck can be towed out.
- **Power and layout:** an emergency power shut-off near the entrance or further away; mark Li-ion charging areas for first responders; keep combustibles, cables and cable trays out of the space above chargers; never park trucks near highly combustible storage.
- **Ventilation:** air exchange of at least one room volume per hour, tied to the chargers so they cut power on ventilation failure; natural ventilation needs at least 400 cm2 of opening per 50 m3 at both floor and ceiling levels.
- **Chargers and batteries:** trucks should have a smart BMS that communicates with the charger to set the correct maximum level and charge rate; use the truck maker's (OEM) charger.
- **Not addressed:** supervision and unattended charging; battery certification; any named standard (it refers only to OEM instructions and fire brigade approval of extinguishing methods).
- **Caveats:** the publisher's jurisdiction is not stated in the retrieved text. The units and fire class (m, cm2, EI60) suggest a European origin; the publisher is, from general knowledge not shown in the text, a Nordic insurance group. This is one insurer's guidance, not a code, and says nothing about US practice.
