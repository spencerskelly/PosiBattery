# Document - HDI Risk Engineering Guideline Battery Charging (2016)

## Definition

An insurer risk-engineering guideline on charging industrial-truck batteries: charging room walls, ventilation, clearances, detection and electrical protection, written around lead-acid batteries.

## Notes

**How it was read:** through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand), not the raw PDF. No version number is stated.

- **Scope:** chargers for industrial trucks and cleaning equipment; electric road vehicles excluded. Charging installations are treated as a fire and explosion hazard area, usually under ATEX-type rules (hydrogen).
- **Room:** charge only in charging rooms or dedicated stations; separated from adjacent areas by a 90-minute fire-resistant wall; sufficiently ventilated (refers to EN 50272-3); free of unneeded fire load; mark each charging point permanently (for example yellow floor hatching).
- **Clearances:** at least 2.50 m horizontal from combustibles, or a non-combustible non-heat-conductive wall at least 1.50 m taller than charger and vehicle; at least 1 m between battery and charger; no combustibles above or below chargers; mechanical collision protection.
- **Detection and extinguishing:** batteries are often charged unattended, so use automatic fire detection (smoke aspiration preferred) and monitor charging and ventilation; CO2 extinguishers preferred over powder; ISO 7010 signs W026, W012, P003.
- **Electrical:** supply-side overcurrent and short-circuit protection plus an RCCB of 300 mA or less (30 mA recommended); charge lines at least 10 mm2; annual charger inspection, daily visual cable checks, thermographic inspection.
- **Lithium:** the guideline says lithium-ion batteries exist and are lighter but that electric vehicles normally use lead-acid; it gives no lithium-specific charging or safety differences. It must not be read as lithium guidance.
- **Date conflict inside the source:** the imprint reference suggests June 2016; the file name suggests 2017-08-14. Neither was confirmed.
