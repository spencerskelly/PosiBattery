# Wireless and Hands-Free Charging Market Notes

## Definition

Running list of points worth keeping about wireless and other hands-free charging products, each marked as owner-stated or evidence-backed, with the notes that hold the detail.

## Notes

### Owner-stated (2026-10-14, not yet backed by a public source)

- ACT is moving ahead with Resonant Link products. Public evidence so far: a 2025-02-10 ACT release saying it is integrating the technology, and no later ACT news item ([[Document - ACT News Page (retrieved 2026-10-14)]]). Related: [[Resonant Link Wireless Lift Truck Charger]], C259, C261.
- Delta MOOV products are already in the market. Public evidence: Delta's catalog lists MOOVair 01, 03, 10 and 30 and the wired MOOVbase and MOOVon lines ([[Document - Delta Industrial Battery Charging Category Page (retrieved 2026-10-14)]]); a 2026 article reports MOOVair 10 and 30 on Linde-based autonomous vehicles ([[Document - Auto Innovations Delta Neumaier Inductive Charging Article]]). The owner statement is consistent with this.
- ACT bought a wireless charging company (owner statement). Public evidence: only a Motive Companies quote about the 'acquisition of ... Resonant Link' with no buyer or terms (C261).

### Interesting points (evidence-backed)

1. Wireless charging reaches forklift-class voltages: EnerSys NexSys AIR has an 80 V, 10 kW converter ([[EnerSys NexSys AIR Wireless Charger]]); Delta MOOVair 30 is 100 V (72-120 V), 300 A, 30 kW ([[Delta MOOVair Wireless Charging Series]]).
2. Air gap ranges widely: 20-50 mm (NexSys AIR), 10-30 mm (MOOVair 03), up to 185 mm (MOOVair 10), 10 in (Resonant Link, vendor figure). See [[Inductive Wireless Charging Product Register]].
3. No OEM-factory wireless option was found for Toyota, Raymond, Crown, Hyster, Yale or Linde Material Handling; routes found are integrators and dealers ([[Lindig L.inductive Charging Offer]]) and projects built on Linde trucks. See [[Forklift Battery Integration and Charging Interface Register]].
4. Toyota Material Handling North America invested in ACT in October 2025; neither release links the investment to wireless charging ([[Advanced Charging Technologies]]).
5. Delta sells all three charging paths under the MOOV name: wireless (MOOVair), wired modular (MOOVbase) and onboard (MOOVon) ([[Delta MOOVbase Modular Charging Systems]], [[Delta MOOVon Onboard Charger and Converters]]).
6. Contact (conductive) hands-free charging for 80/96 V trucks still has no published product; wireless and the Stäubli QCC (up to 1,500 V) are the only hands-free routes with ratings above 60 V ([[Charger-Vehicle Interface Register]], IB-206).
7. Delta publishes data sheets for its pad systems; most other wireless makers publish less than half the fields, so comparisons rely on trade press (T2).
