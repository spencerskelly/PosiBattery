## Notes

What the text says, quoted or paraphrased closely from the file:

- **Article 3 definitions:** (13) industrial battery = designed for industrial uses, or any other battery over 5 kg that is not an electric vehicle, LMT or SLI battery. (14) electric vehicle battery = traction battery for category L vehicles over 25 kg or for categories M, N or O road vehicles. (11) LMT battery = sealed, 25 kg or less, light vehicles. (27) state of charge = available energy as a percentage of rated capacity as declared by the manufacturer. (28) state of health = a measure of the general condition of a rechargeable battery and its ability to deliver the specified performance compared with its initial condition. (25) battery management system = an electronic device that controls or manages the electric and thermal functions of a battery to ensure safety, performance and service life, manages and stores the data for the Annex VII parameters, and communicates with the vehicle, light vehicle or appliance, or with charging infrastructure.
- **Recital 15:** traction batteries for rail, waterborne, aviation or off-road machinery stay in the industrial category. Forklifts are not named. Reading a forklift traction battery as an industrial battery is analyst reasoning from this recital, not a quoted statement.
- **No chemistry exclusion found:** text search for lead returned only recycled-content and recovery provisions; the passport (Article 77) and the performance document (Article 10) are defined by category and the 2 kWh threshold, not chemistry.
- **Article 10(1):** from 2024-08-18 rechargeable industrial batteries over 2 kWh, LMT and electric vehicle batteries must be accompanied by a document with the Annex IV Part A values: rated capacity (Ah) and capacity fade (%), power (W) and power fade, internal resistance and its increase, round trip efficiency and fade where applicable, expected lifetime in cycles and calendar years. Part B asks for the measurement conditions: charge and discharge rates, depth of discharge in the cycle-life test, power at 80 and 20 percent state of charge. Article 10(2) and (5): minimum values for industrial batteries over 2 kWh apply from 2027-08-18 or 18 months after the delegated act, which the text says the Commission shall adopt by 2026-02-18. Whether it was adopted is not in this file.
- **Article 14(1):** from 2024-08-18 up-to-date Annex VII data shall be contained in the battery management system of **stationary battery energy storage systems, LMT batteries and electric vehicle batteries**. Industrial batteries that are not stationary storage systems are not named in this paragraph. Article 14(2): read-only access for the purchaser, including independent operators, for residual value, remaining lifetime, re-use, repurposing and remanufacturing. Article 14(3): the BMS must include a software reset function.
- **Annex VII Part A:** for electric vehicle batteries the state of health parameter is the state of certified energy (SOCE); for stationary storage and LMT batteries it is remaining capacity, and where possible remaining power capability, round trip efficiency, evolution of self-discharge rate and ohmic resistance. **Part B (expected lifetime, stationary and LMT):** date of manufacture and putting into service, energy throughput, capacity throughput, tracking of harmful events (deep discharge events, time in extreme temperatures, time charging in extreme temperatures), number of full equivalent cycles.
- **Article 77(1):** from 2027-02-18 each LMT battery, **each industrial battery with a capacity greater than 2 kWh** and each electric vehicle battery has a battery passport. The economic operator placing the battery on the market keeps it accurate, complete and up to date and may authorise another operator to act for it (77(4)). For re-used, repurposed or remanufactured batteries responsibility transfers to the operator that places or puts it into service, with a new passport linked to the old (77(7)). Article 77(9): the Commission shall adopt by 2026-08-18 an implementing act on who has a legitimate interest and what they may access. Article 13(6): QR code on batteries from 2027-02-18; the code and identifier follow ISO/IEC 15459 parts 1 to 6 (77(3)).
- **Article 78:** the data are stored by the responsible operator or those it authorises, who may not sell or reuse them beyond providing the storage service; access follows Annex XIII and the 77(9) act; data authentication, reliability and integrity must be ensured.
- **Annex XIII point 1 (public, per model):** items (a) to (s), including rated capacity in Ah, minimum, nominal and maximum voltage with temperature ranges, original power capability, expected lifetime in cycles with the reference test, capacity threshold for exhaustion (electric vehicle batteries only), internal cell and pack resistance, c-rate of the cycle-life test.
- **Annex XIII point 4 (per individual battery, persons with a legitimate interest):** (a) Article 10(1) performance and durability values at placing on the market and when status changes; (b) state of health information "pursuant to Article 14"; (c) status (original, repurposed, re-used, remanufactured, waste); (d) data from use: number of charge and discharge cycles, negative events such as accidents, and **periodically recorded information on operating environmental conditions including temperature, and on the state of charge**.
