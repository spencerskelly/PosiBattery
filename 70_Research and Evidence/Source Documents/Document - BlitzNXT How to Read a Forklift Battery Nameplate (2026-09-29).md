## Notes

**Content recorded (as stated by the source, not independently verified unless marked):**
- Lead-acid forklift batteries are rated at the 6-hour discharge rate; lithium packs are more commonly specified at 0.2 C (5-hour equivalent), so Ah values are not directly comparable across chemistries.
- Compartment fit: clearance typically a quarter to half an inch per side. The battery is also counterweight, so a lighter battery can reduce rated lifting capacity; the post says lithium is typically 30 to 50 percent lighter at equivalent energy and that a swap may need ballast or an engineering check (C180).
- Cites 29 CFR 1910.178 for securing batteries against contact with compartment walls and says the truck's data plate states the minimum battery weight.
- Lead-acid code such as 18-85-17: first number cells (2 V each, 18 cells = 36 V), second 'plate design or type code', third plates per cell (odd). Compare the convention the vault derived from the Fleetman chart (C182).
- Mistakes list: assuming Ah is comparable across chemistries, ordering by voltage only, ignoring minimum weight, ignoring connector type, relying only on the old battery nameplate.

**Checks made:**
- Plate code first and third numbers agree with the Fleetman chart (18 cells, 36 V). The second number is described vaguely; see C182.
