# California Forklift Electrification Regulations Register

## Definition

Register of the California rules that push or reward forklift electrification and that touch charger design and data: CARB's Zero-Emission Forklift (ZEF) regulation and the Low Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS) forklift reporting rules. It records who is bound, dates, enforcement status, conflicts between sources and what each means for chargers and their data. Evidence sits on the Document notes; disagreements are numbered C279 to C290 in [[Battery Product Landscape Conflicts and Open Questions]].

## Notes

- **Reading rule:** every source was read through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand). CARB and EPA publications sit outside the T1 to T5 tiers (see each Document note); trade and consultancy sources are T2 or T4. "Analyst reasoning" marks a conclusion no source states. Status is as of 2026-10-17; no CARB update after the April 2026 brochure was found.
- **Scope of this pass:** only the ZEF and LCFS rows of the 2026-10-04 list in [[Knowledge Base Backlog]] were researched. The other rows (EU battery passport, cyber rules, tariffs, NFPA 855, cellular sunsets and so on) are still unverified.

### Bottom line (analyst summary)

1. **ZEF is enforced today only against state and local government fleets** (compliance since 2026-01-01; phase-out 2028 to 2038). Sources: [[Document - CARB ZEF Government Fleets Overview Fact Sheet (2026-01)]], [[Document - CARB ZEF Brochure for State and Local Government Fleets (2026-04)]].
2. **For private fleets and for forklift sellers the rule is paused, not repealed.** CARB staff will not enforce it until EPA authorizes it or finds authorization unnecessary, and CARB's notices through January 2026 still say it has not asked EPA. Sources: [[Document - CARB ZEF Implementation Update Advisory (2025-05-29)]], [[Document - CARB ZEF Authorization Update Bulletin (2025-05-27)]], [[Document - CARB ZEF Government Fleets Overview Fact Sheet (2026-01)]]. A 2028-or-later private-fleet phase-out should be a scenario, not baseline demand (analyst reasoning).
3. **LCFS forklift credits now need metered kWh.** From Q1 2026 reports CARB no longer accepts estimates; a meter within 5 percent is needed, and the facility owner is the first reporting entity (since 2025-07-01). Claiming credits is optional. Source: [[Document - CARB LCFS Guidance 25-01 Forklift Fueling Reporting (2025-10)]].
4. **Two statements in the 2026-10-04 backlog need correction** (table below): the phase-out applies to government fleets only, and Guidance 20-03 is not the metering source.

### Charger-side evidence already in the vault

- Charger efficiency values (vendor claims, mostly peak or unspecified) are in [[Metric - Peak Efficiency]] and [[Metric - Full-Cycle Efficiency]]; the 19 charger entries listed in the peak note run from 90 to 97 percent.
- [[PosiCharge E-Meter]] is a battery-compartment device that records energy and usage data (sheet: analysis includes billing discrepancies); no source gives its measurement accuracy or says it is used for LCFS reporting.
- [[PosiCharge PosiLink]] uses site data including E-Meter and IC-Meter data; the IC-Meter has no note or sheet yet ([[Unidentified Products Review]]).
- No vault note states a charger's kWh measurement accuracy or a per-location kWh export.

### Implications for chargers and data (analyst reasoning, hypotheses only)

- **H1, publish a charger efficiency.** If the meter is upstream of the charger, reportable energy is metered kWh times the charger's efficiency, or 85 percent if none is listed. Derived effect for PosiCharge entries in the vault, per 100 kWh metered: DVS300 and MVS400 powerserver at 90 percent give 90 kWh (5.9 percent above the default); SVS100 at 91 percent gives 91 kWh (7.1 percent); DVS150 at up to 92 percent gives 92 kWh (8.2 percent); MVS800 powerserver, ProCore Solo (up to) and High Voltage Power Station (AC) at 95 percent give 95 kWh (11.8 percent). This assumes the sheet figure is what the guidance means and that one number is accepted; the guidance does not say whether peak or full-cycle efficiency is meant (C287).
- **H2, charger-output kWh.** The guidance accepts a meter at the charger output within 5 percent. A charger that reports kWh per port or per session could let a site owner meet metering without extra hardware, but the guidance does not say whether charger-reported data qualifies (C287). No source shows any PosiCharge product's accuracy.
- **H3, report per location.** The facility owner registers each charging location as fueling supply equipment and reports for it; a platform that totals kWh by location and keeps meter records would match what the owner must file. No vault note shows this today.
- **H4, evidence for verifiers.** From the 2026 data year a third party verifies reports (statements due August 2027). The guidance names meter serial numbers and rental agreements as records to keep, so serial-numbered meters with calibration history would help.
- **H5, utilities see regulated load.** The ZEF rule makes government fleets contact their utility by location; see the update on [[Electric Utility or Grid Operator]]. No PosiCharge product interface to a utility is evidenced.
- **H6, sizing.** California has more than 89,000 spark-ignited forklifts (CARB), but only the government share is currently forced to change; private-fleet electrification is an economic choice today (analyst reasoning).

### What would change this picture

- CARB asks EPA for authorization under section 209(e)(2)(A), or EPA says it is unnecessary; CARB then publishes a notice ([[Document - CARB ZEF Implementation Update Advisory (2025-05-29)]]).
- The settlement terms end or are changed (WPGA says they run for the current federal administration; [[Document - WPGA Settlement Press Release on ZEF (2025-05-27)]]).
- CARB publishes new forklift rulemaking; WPGA says it would hold at least three public workshops first.
- CARB answers whether charger-reported kWh and which efficiency figure qualify (C287).
- The adopted EER table shows a different forklift value than 3.8 (C286).

### Open questions

Tracked as C286 to C289 in [[Battery Product Landscape Conflicts and Open Questions]] and IB-242 to IB-251 in [[Investigation Backlog]]: the adopted forklift EER, the CARB answer on charger data and efficiency basis, whether utilities can still claim unreported forklift electricity, whether the 2012-authorized fleet rule still binds private fleets, other states' clean fuel programs, credit economics, and ZEF sizing.
