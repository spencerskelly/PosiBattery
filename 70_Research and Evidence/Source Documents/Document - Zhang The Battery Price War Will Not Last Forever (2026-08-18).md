# Document - Zhang The Battery Price War Will Not Last Forever (2026-08-18)

## Definition

Argues that battery competition will move from price to total cost of ownership and system features.

## Provenance

- **Local artifact:** `(1) The Battery Price War Will Not Last Forever _ LinkedIn.pdf` in `Reference Files`.
- **Author or publisher:** Maggie Zhang, LinkedIn newsletter 'Powering Industrial Mobility'. One article in the series names the author's company as RuiDeJin New Energy, a maker of customized lithium packs, so the series is supplier-authored.

## Notes

**Content recorded (as stated by the source, not independently verified unless marked):**
- States that in 2025 the global average lithium-ion pack price reached about 108 USD/kWh and about 84 USD/kWh in China, citing BloombergNEF. Verified against the BloombergNEF release (see the register).
- Says BloombergNEF expects further reductions in 2026 and that the IEA has pointed to continued cost pressure.
- Total cost is purchase price plus charging cost, maintenance, downtime, replacement and service. Example: a battery 10 percent cheaper can lose the saving if it has lower usable energy, more protection events or earlier replacement.
- Predicts differentiation moving to smart BMS, remote monitoring, SOC and SOH accuracy, predictive maintenance, CAN communication, OTA updates, thermal management, data traceability and lifecycle management.
- Questions buyers will ask: communication with the AGV, real peak current, behavior at -20 C, BMS behavior after five years, remote monitoring, mechanical modification, certification data, field failure response.

**Checks made:**
- BloombergNEF figures confirmed on the primary release dated 2025-12-09. The release gives no forklift or industrial-truck pack price, so the 108 and 84 USD/kWh figures are market averages, not forklift battery prices.
