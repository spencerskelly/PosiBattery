# OEM In-House Lithium Battery and Charger Offers Register

## Definition

Register of what truck makers (OEMs) say they make and sell themselves in lithium-ion batteries and chargers, whether they still approve third-party power, and what that could mean for third-party charger and battery-adjacent products such as PosiCharge's. It checks the 2026-10-04 backlog row on OEM-integrated battery and charger offers ([[Knowledge Base Backlog]]). Disagreements are numbered C323 to C327 in [[Battery Product Landscape Conflicts and Open Questions]]; new directions are IB-284 to IB-291 in [[Investigation Backlog]].

## Notes

- **Reading rule:** every source was read through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand). Maker pages and press releases are marketing claims. "Analyst reasoning" marks a conclusion no source states. Status is as of 2026-10-17.
- **Scope:** statements by Hyster-Yale, Toyota (and Raymond), Jungheinrich, KION (Linde and STILL) and Crown. Mitsubishi Logisnext (Triathlon supply) and Raymond's own pages are already covered in [[Industrial Battery Supply and Private-Label Relationships]] and are not re-read.
- **Not covered:** any PosiCharge sales, installed-base or win/loss data (none in the vault; KB-010).

### Bottom line (analyst summary)

1. **The backlog premise is partly right but not new.** Hyster-Yale is the latest, not the first, large truck maker to sell its own lithium battery and charger: Jungheinrich (2017), Toyota Europe (2018), KION's own battery plant (batteries since 2019), Crown (V-Force, undated page) and now Hyster-Yale (2026) all say so in their own material (C323).
2. **Hyster-Yale's change is a business-model change, not a product line.** An executive says it has "generally sold trucks without" batteries and now supplies "the battery" and "the charger" itself, with higher revenue and margin (Q4 2025 call); a 2025 release says it will develop and manufacture modules, chargers and BMS at its Billerica site. Shipping started in Europe, with North America planned for the start of Q3 2026 per a secondary summary (C325, IB-285).
3. **OEMs still approve third-party power.** Hyster-Yale approved Stryten's Li600 for Power Cellect in June 2025 (that battery pairs with Stryten's chargers); Linde approved UgoWork in April 2026 while KION builds its own (C323). So the claim "turnkey from a single source" describes an offer, not an exclusion of third parties; no source says warranty or truck operation requires the OEM charger (IB-289, IB-290).
4. **Who is exposed is unknown.** The segments where a third-party charger is sold to a customer who also buys an OEM-validated pack are the exposed ones (analyst reasoning). No data on PosiCharge's mix exists in the vault.
5. **C18 is not settled by this pass.** The 2026 Hyster release names Hyster Tracker telemetry fed by the battery management system and does not mention PosiCharge (C327). The PosiCharge-powered Hyster Battery Tracker may remain relevant to lead-acid and third-party battery trucks; that is untested.
6. **Vault gap closed, with a catch.** The vault said "not found: who makes Toyota's lithium-ion batteries"; a 2024 trade article says Toyota and Raymond make lithium-ion and pure-lead batteries in a New York plant (C324). It does not say that the packs now sold come from that plant, and it names no chargers.

### Hypotheses (analyst reasoning, untested)

- **OH1:** OEM bundling of battery, charger and telemetry gives OEMs a revenue and margin pull that raises with each launch, so third-party charger volume at OEM lithium trucks shrinks while lead-acid and mixed fleets stay open. Test: IB-290 and customer win/loss data (KB-010).
- **OH2:** third-party approval programs (Power Cellect, Linde approvals) exist because OEMs cannot serve every customer, and they will tighten as the OEM's own supply scales. Test: IB-289.
- **OH3:** the OEM-integrated offer makes the charger-to-BMS interface (CAN) the deciding feature for third-party chargers, which supports the vault's BMID and BMS work. Test: IB-289.

### Decision for the owner

Is the business question here "defend the OEM charger channel" or "supply the OEM"? Supplying an OEM is a possible route: Delta-Q states it supplies chargers to OEMs ([[Industrial Battery Supply and Private-Label Relationships]]). But Hyster-Yale says it builds its own chargers, which closes that route there; Jungheinrich said the same in 2017. Toyota, KION and Crown were not shown to build their own chargers in the sources read.

## Open questions

- Who builds Hyster's battery cells and chargers, and what are the models, voltages and prices (IB-284)?
- Do OEM warranties require the OEM charger (IB-290)?
- What share of PosiCharge business sits at Hyster, Yale, Toyota, Raymond, Crown and KION trucks? No data.
