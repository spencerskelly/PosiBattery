# Cost Driver 05: MHE Lifecycle Cost

## Executive summary

Material-handling equipment (MHE) lifecycle cost includes acquisition or lease expense, scheduled and corrective maintenance, batteries and chargers, energy, damage, rentals, downtime, administration, and replacement timing. The strongest commercial products reduce these costs by combining equipment telemetry, utilization analytics, maintenance workflows, battery intelligence, and enterprise cost reporting.

Products used by businesses today include Crown InfoLink, Raymond iWAREHOUSE Evolution and iTRACK, Toyota I_Site, Powerfleet On-Site, Kenco managed fleet services, MaintainX and Fiix CMMS platforms, PosiCharge Battery Rx/PosiGuard/PosiLink, EnerSys Wi-iQ, and Fronius Charge & Connect. They solve different layers of the problem: truck telematics measures use and behavior; CMMS products execute maintenance; battery platforms protect the energy asset; and managed-fleet services optimize sourcing, service, and replacement.

Documented examples show the available value. Powerfleet reports that Kautex reduced one site's fleet by more than 10%, saving more than $10,000 annually for each truck eliminated; Kenco reports a 26% reduction in MHE spend for Sealed Air in the first year; and Powerfleet reports a global manufacturer reduced its fleet by 7% while also reducing damage and labor cost.[cite:482][cite:487][cite:106]

## Cost structure

| Cost element | Typical waste | Product mechanism | Financial effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acquisition and lease | Excess units, wrong truck type, duplicated standby capacity | Utilization, concurrency and scenario analytics | Avoided purchases, leases, tax, insurance and depreciation |
| Preventive maintenance | Calendar service on lightly used assets; missed service on hard-used assets | Accurate run hours and usage-triggered work orders | Fewer unnecessary visits and failures caused by missed service |
| Corrective maintenance | Late detection, poor fault context, repeat repairs | Fault capture, condition monitoring and remote diagnostics | Lower emergency labor, parts, travel and consequential damage |
| Downtime | Waiting for diagnosis, technician, approval or parts | Automated alerts, dispatch, asset status and parts reservation | Higher availability; less rental and spare capacity |
| Damage and abuse | Impacts, overload, harsh use and anonymous operation | Operator authentication, impact sensing, coaching and lockout | Lower truck, rack, building, load and battery damage |
| Battery and charger | Heat, low water, imbalance, misuse and premature replacement | Battery telemetry, charger connectivity, rotation and load management | Longer asset life and fewer energy-related outages |
| Energy and fuel | Idling, inefficient charging and demand peaks | Energy metering, idle analytics and charging controls | Lower electricity, demand, propane or fuel expense |
| Administration | Manual meters, spreadsheets and fragmented service records | Automatic collection, CMMS integration and standardized reporting | Less administrative labor and better spend control |
| Replacement timing | Repairing uneconomic assets or replacing useful assets early | Unit-level cost, downtime, utilization and risk analysis | Better capital allocation and lifecycle economics |

A useful unit-level model is:

`Annual MHE TCO = lease/depreciation + maintenance labor + parts + battery/charger allocation + energy + damage + rentals + downtime + administration - residual value`

The two complementary decision metrics are **cost per available hour** and **cost per productive move**. Cost per hour alone can reward idle equipment; utilization alone can hide excessive repair, damage or energy cost.

## Needs and value

### Fleet right-sizing

Customers need to know the truck requirement by class, site, shift and peak interval—not average key-on count. Useful products distinguish travel, lift, idle, loaded, empty, charging, maintenance and unavailable states and analyze peak concurrency.

Powerfleet reports that Kautex removed more than 10% of the fleet at its first analyzed facility, producing more than $10,000 annual cost reduction per eliminated truck.[cite:482] Another Powerfleet case reports a 7% reduction and approximately $500,000 annual fleet savings across about 800 vehicles at five plants.[cite:106] Crown Packaging used InfoLink utilization and opportunity-charging data to reduce its fleet and avoid spare trucks used only for charging coverage.[cite:491][cite:493]

**Functions:** runtime-state separation, peak concurrency, asset relocation/removal scenarios, equipment-class compatibility, seasonal demand and battery readiness.

**Productivity:** fewer peak shortages, improved departmental allocation and less time locating available equipment.

**Costs reduced:** lease/depreciation, service contracts, batteries, chargers, insurance, tax, parts, floor space and rentals.

### Maintenance planning

Customers need service based on risk and usage while retaining required OEM and safety work. Products create value by combining runtime, fault codes, inspection failures, condition indicators, repair history, parts and technician availability.

Crown supports PM scheduling from hour readings, automatic service notification, event-code transmission and downtime records.[cite:513] Raymond iTRACK tracks service, work orders, maintenance cost and response time.[cite:515] MaintainX and Fiix add cross-vendor execution, inventory, procedures, approvals and analytics.[cite:498][cite:511]

**Functions:** meter- and condition-triggered work, automatic CMMS work orders, remote triage, parts reservation, digital procedures, repeat-failure detection and warranty evidence.

**Productivity:** higher planned-work ratio, shorter diagnosis, higher first-time fix and lower mean time to repair (MTTR).

**Costs reduced:** unnecessary service, catastrophic failure, emergency labor, after-hours calls, rush freight, repeat repair and warranty leakage.

### Battery lifecycle

The operational requirement is assured energy availability aligned with workload. A degraded battery can remove a healthy truck from service, create hidden spare-capacity requirements and consume operator time.

Toyota reports that Kuehne + Nagel used I_Site battery data to determine that it held too many spare batteries and changing tables, enabling battery-room reorganization and asset reduction.[cite:484] PosiCharge and Fronius products address health, charging behavior, rotation and charger availability.[cite:523][cite:524]

**Functions:** battery-truck-charger association, SOC/SOH and thermal monitoring, shift-readiness prediction, equalization/watering workflows, charger assignment, remaining-life forecast and demand-aware charging.

**Productivity:** fewer mid-shift events, lower search/change time and more predictable truck availability.

**Costs reduced:** batteries, changing equipment, charger service, peak demand, emergency rentals and warranty disputes.

### Damage and abuse

Effective systems connect an impact to the operator, asset, location, severity, inspection and corrective action rather than merely count shocks.

Powerfleet reports that a food and beverage company reduced forklift damage cost 85% while meeting 100% of target pallet moves, producing more than $2 million in enterprise savings.[cite:108][cite:477] Raymond reports Masters Gallery Foods reduced impact alerts 88% in five months after data-driven operator training.[cite:494] Toyota reports Dagrofa Logistik's truck-damage rate fell from 14% in 2018 to 3.2% in 2021 following I_Site deployment, although telematics cannot be isolated as the sole cause.[cite:484]

**Functions:** calibrated impact detection, operator identity, notification, controlled lockout, post-impact inspection, hotspot analytics and coaching closure.

**Productivity:** fewer withdrawals and investigations while maintaining material velocity.

**Costs reduced:** truck, rack, door, product, battery and facility damage; downtime and claims.

### Replacement decisions

Customers need a consistent threshold for repairing, rebuilding, relocating, selling or replacing equipment.

**Functions:** 12- and 36-month cost trends, cost per productive hour, repeat-failure analysis, keep/overhaul/lease/buy scenarios, residual value, lead time, warranty and criticality scoring.

**Productivity:** removal of chronic-failure assets and better application fit.

**Costs reduced:** uneconomic repairs, premature replacement, parts proliferation and poor lease-end decisions.

## KPI framework

| KPI | Definition | Decision supported |
|---|---|---|
| Asset availability | Available scheduled hours / scheduled hours | Maintenance and spare-capacity requirement |
| Productive utilization | Loaded travel plus lift/task time / available hours | Right-sizing and allocation |
| Peak concurrency | Peak or percentile simultaneously productive units | Capacity reduction without service risk |
| MTBF | Productive hours between functional failures | Reliability improvement |
| MTTR | Failure/request to return-to-service time | Diagnosis, dispatch and parts delays |
| PM compliance | On-time PMs / PMs due | Maintenance execution quality |
| First-time fix | Repairs without recurrence in a defined interval | Diagnostic and repair effectiveness |
| Battery readiness | Assets meeting next-shift energy need / assets required | Energy-system capacity |
| Maintenance cost/run hour | Labor + parts + fees / run hours | Asset comparison |
| Lifecycle cost/move | Annualized TCO / verified productive moves | Technology and fleet-design comparison |
| Damage cost/1,000 hours | All impact-related cost / run hours × 1,000 | Behavior and facility interventions |
| Energy cost/productive hour | Electricity or fuel / productive hours | Powertrain and charging choices |

## Product packages

### Fleet census and TCO

**Core:** universal asset registry, runtime/utilization, imported lease/maintenance/energy/damage costs, asset rankings and right-sizing recommendations.

**Options:** peak-concurrency scenarios, rental-versus-buy analysis, lease-renewal workflow, residual value, cross-site benchmarking and carbon reporting.

**Value:** avoids excess fleet capital, exposes expensive assets and creates a defensible capital plan.

### Connected maintenance

**Core:** accurate run hours, PM alerts, fault/inspection capture, CMMS work orders, downtime reason and repair-cost history.

**Options:** vibration/temperature/current/pressure sensors, remote diagnostics, parts and technician matching, explainable risk scoring and post-repair validation.

**Value:** shifts work from emergency to planned activity, lowers MTTR and prevents consequential failure.

### Energy-asset lifecycle

**Core:** battery identity, truck/charger association, SOC, temperature, voltage, current, charger status, alerts and shift readiness.

**Options:** multi-chemistry SOH, water and imbalance, rotation, charger assignment, demand control, life forecast, tariffs and PV-aware scheduling.

**Value:** reduces battery replacement, truck downtime, peak demand, inspection labor and spare capacity.

### Damage and behavior

**Core:** operator identity, certification, impact sensing, notifications, electronic inspections and trend reporting.

**Options:** UWB/BLE location, event video, geofenced modes, coaching workflow and linkage to rack/product damage cases.

**Value:** reduces damage without relying on indiscriminate speed limits that may hurt throughput.

### Enterprise optimization

**Core:** multi-site dashboards, common KPI definitions, APIs to WMS/LMS/CMMS/ERP/identity and exception routing.

**Options:** workload-normalized benchmarks, fleet/charging digital twin, automatic capacity reservation, supplier scorecards and outcome-based contracts.

**Value:** converts separate data products into an operating system for lifecycle decisions and auditable savings.

## Architecture requirements

The data model should treat truck, battery, charger, operator, location and attachment as separate entities with time-valid relationships. A battery event has limited value if the system cannot determine which truck, charger, operator and shift were associated with it at that moment.

Integration priorities are:

1. CMMS/EAM assets, work orders, downtime, parts and labor.
2. WMS/LMS tasks, moves, operator identity and paid time.
3. Truck CAN/OEM telemetry for hours, state, fault and impact.
4. Battery and charger data for readiness, stress, energy and lifecycle.
5. ERP/finance for leases, invoices, depreciation and capital.
6. Identity/certification for access and accountability.
7. Utility meter and tariff data for charging optimization.

Edge requirements include store-and-forward operation, local safety functions, secure device identity, signed firmware, authenticated configuration, time synchronization, data-quality flags and privacy controls.

## Deployment guide

### Selection questions

- Which OEMs, truck classes, batteries and chargers are supported, and is control bidirectional?
- Are run, lift, idle, load, charge and unavailable states measured directly or inferred?
- Can an alert create, route, acknowledge and close a work order?
- Are APIs documented for the customer's WMS, CMMS, ERP and identity systems?
- Does the product calculate unit-level TCO and preserve savings evidence?
- Who installs, calibrates and supports devices, and under what SLA?
- How are devices authenticated, updated and segmented; who owns the data?
- Can common KPIs and policies operate across sites?
- Which outcomes have named customers, baselines, denominators and time periods?

### Pilot method

1. Collect 8–12 weeks of baseline data covering availability, moves, paid hours, maintenance, damage, batteries, energy, rentals and concurrency.
2. Select comparable treatment and reference areas where practical.
3. Predefine actions: remove or relocate equipment, change PM triggers, connect CMMS alerts, change battery assignment or coach events.
4. Measure adoption: acknowledgment, closure, login, inspection and recommended-action completion.
5. Include a representative high-demand interval before removing critical capacity.
6. Have finance distinguish realized cash savings from avoided or modeled cost.
7. Require exit criteria for data quality, system uptime, cybersecurity, workflow closure and payback.

## ROI model

Annual benefits may include avoided fleet cost, maintenance labor and parts, rentals, overtime, battery replacements, damage, energy demand, administration and accepted warranty claims. Costs include hardware, installation, SaaS, connectivity, integration, calibration, support, training, change management and cybersecurity.

`Payback months = total implementation cost / verified monthly net benefit`

Do not treat lower idle time as a financial saving unless it changes labor, fleet count, overtime, throughput or service performance.

## Risks and limitations

- **Telemetry without action:** every important event needs an owner, required action, response target, closure evidence and escalation.
- **Vendor evidence:** most MHE case studies are supplier-authored and combine multiple interventions; use them to form hypotheses, not forecast guaranteed savings.
- **Over-maintenance:** poorly tuned alerts can increase work; track precision, false positives and avoided failures.
- **Right-sizing risk:** include peak demand, attachments, battery charging, maintenance coverage, seasonality and service requirements before removing units.
- **Vendor lock-in:** contract for raw-data access, documented export, API continuity, retention and post-contract access.
- **Safety and cybersecurity:** lockout, speed and charging controls require fail-safe local behavior, authorization, audit logs and hazard analysis.
- **Operator trust:** normalize individual metrics for congestion, task difficulty, equipment condition and training to reduce gaming and unfair conclusions.

## Recommended roadmap

1. **Normalize assets and cost:** establish truck-battery-charger identity, runtime and unit-level lifecycle cost.
2. **Close maintenance workflows:** route alarms and failed checks into CMMS with acknowledgment and closure.
3. **Add shift readiness:** convert battery and charger telemetry into operational capacity forecasts.
4. **Verify savings:** track each recommendation, customer action, operational result and financial approval.
5. **Optimize fleet and charging:** add concurrency, charger-capacity and energy scheduling models.
6. **Expand condition coverage:** add OEM/CAN and external sensors only where failure cost justifies complexity.
7. **Support enterprise benchmarking:** normalize for workload and site type before comparing facilities.
