# Document - MHI Blog Navigating Third-Party Safety Standards for Lithium Batteries in Lift Trucks (2024-10-08)

## Definition

Trade-association blog summarizing a MODEX 2024 panel on UL 2580, UL 583, UL 1564 and related standards for lithium batteries in lift trucks, in the panelists' words.

## Notes

**How it was read:** through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand). The statements below are panelists' claims relayed by the blog, not standards text.

- **UL 2580:** one panelist (UgoWork) calls it the "umbrella certification" lift truck makers use for lithium batteries and says it is still being adapted and UL discussed splitting it; the summary found his comments on whether it covers lithium contradictory.
- **UL 583 (electric lift trucks, classes 1 to 3):** a Hyster-Yale panelist says it was written for technology that predates lithium and that truck makers are reviewing how to add lithium. UL 558 is the internal-combustion counterpart.
- **UL 1564 (chargers):** a panelist says UL no longer maintains it but still tests to it, and that OSHA expects warehouse chargers to be certified to it. Conflicts with the ANSI listing of a 5th edition dated 2024-02-13 ([[Document - ANSI Webstore UL 1564 Ed 5-2024 Listing (retrieved 2026-10-17)]], C316). The OSHA expectation is a panelist's statement; no OSHA text was read.
- **ANSI B56.1 and ITSDF:** 1910.178 references them for truck design (the blog writes "B65.1" once, apparently a typo for B56.1).
- **Who requires what:** a lithium truck needs both the truck's UL label and the battery's UL label; if the battery is not UL-listed the truck label must be removed (Hyster-Yale panelist). Insurers generally impose the same expectations (a panelist). Truck makers sometimes require battery makers to test to UL 2580.
- **Not mentioned:** UL 1973, UL 3100, NFPA, AHJs. The article gives no edition numbers.
