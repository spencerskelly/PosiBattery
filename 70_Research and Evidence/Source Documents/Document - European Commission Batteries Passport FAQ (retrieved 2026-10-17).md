# Document - European Commission Batteries Passport FAQ (retrieved 2026-10-17)

## Definition

Commission FAQ on the battery passport: what counts as the finished battery, who is the responsible operator, identifiers, access rights, dynamic data and state-of-health requirements.

## Notes

**How it was read:** through a summarizing fetch tool (second-hand); numbers below are the FAQ's question numbers as the tool returned them.

- **Scope (3.1):** the passport applies to the finished battery; "for a traction battery, the finished battery would normally include the BMS".
- **Responsible operator (2.1, 2.3, 2.5, 3.5):** the operator placing the battery on the market stays responsible; if a vehicle maker only installs a finished battery the battery maker is responsible; if the vehicle maker completes the battery (for example adds a BMS) it is responsible; for imported products with a battery installed, the importer.
- **Data and timing (7.1 to 7.3, 8.1):** not all fields are mandatory; carbon footprint, recycled content and due diligence information are not yet required; due diligence applies from August 2027; dynamic data requirements are expected to apply from February 2027.
- **State of health (8.3 to 8.5):** dynamic data updated at least on a status change, more often if technically feasible and proportionate; real-time communication not required; "State of health requirements apply only where the battery has a BMS".
- **Access (6.3 to 6.5, 1.4):** the public part is set in Annex XIII point 1; other access depends on a forthcoming implementing act (draft expected October or November 2026); the registry does not hold the full passport.
- **Remanufacture (9.4):** a battery placed on the market before February 2027 needs a passport if remanufactured after that date.
- **Not addressed:** lead-acid, forklifts, QR code and data carrier, retrofit devices.
