# Reduce Operator Effort and Fatigue

## Definition

Customer need: Reduce Operator Effort and Fatigue. The problem behind it: operators steer, step on and off, and walk beside trucks all shift, and effort adds fatigue, slower work and injury risk.

## Notes

- **Evidence status: need hypothesis, analyst-derived.** No vault source states this need as a customer problem. The need was reasoned from what the functions below do and from the work the customer is doing; the product statements are what makers say the features do. No customer, user or buyer source confirms the need or its rank.
- **Customer work this need protects or removes (analyst reasoning):** picking orders on foot beside a truck (Candidate operational Use Case: Pick Orders on Foot Alongside the Truck) and steering a truck through a shift. Follow-the-operator removes the use case of climbing on, driving and stepping off for every pick.
- **Problem solved (analyst wording):** the functions remove physical steps from the work (heavy steering, driving and getting off between picks).
- **Who has the problem:** [[Forklift Operator]], [[Fleet Operations Manager]].
- **Realized by (specific functions; products link to these):** [[Follow Operator Automatically]], [[Steer with Electric Power Assist]].
- **Product-stated evidence:**
  - [[Jungheinrich easyPILOT]]: belt-worn remote control lets a truck follow an operator on foot in order picking. Source: <https://logisticsmatters.co.uk/page_724637.asp> (V).
  - [[Mitsubishi FB 3-Wheel Electric Forklifts]] and [[Mitsubishi FBCS Stand-Up Counterbalanced Forklifts]]: electric power steering that cuts steering effort. Source: <https://www.allmachines.com/forklifts/mitsubishi-fb20pnt> (V), <https://www.allmachines.com/forklifts/mitsubishi-fbcs18n> (V).
- **Gaps:** no source states the effort, fatigue or injury problem; the existing vault holds one follow-the-operator product and two power-steering trucks; no Requirement is linked.
